# Pentatonic Scales

Five-note scales that form some of the most fundamental melodic vocabulary in jazz (and music broadly). The **minor pentatonic** (root, ♭3, 4, 5, ♭7) has a characteristically angular, gapped quality that stands apart from diatonic scalar motion. In jazz, pentatonics are used both as primary melodic material and as superimposed structures over harmony — played from a root that creates tension or colour against the underlying chord.

## Appearances

- **[[../videos/0paCMVrPm_8|Five Levels of Pentatonic Scale Practice]]** [▶ 2:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0paCMVrPm_8&t=154s) — the entire session is built around the C minor pentatonic. Five progressive practice levels are introduced: (1) linear scales from each scale tone, (2) four-note groupings, (3) broken groupings with arch-shaped skips, (4) combined linear runs into broken groups, (5) half-step slides that shift the entire shape chromatically. Each level is treated as a musical phrase rather than a mechanical exercise.
- **[[../videos/-ktm1Q0odH8|Three Jazz Piano Voicing Structures for Lady Bird]]** [▶ 15:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ktm1Q0odH8&t=959s) — pentatonic shapes form the structural basis of the magic voicing system, where the five notes of the major pentatonic are spread between the hands to create rootless ensemble voicings.
