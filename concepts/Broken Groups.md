# Broken Groups

A melodic device where a set of scale tones (typically four) is played not in a straight linear sequence but with an arch-shaped skip: the group ascends partway then dips back before continuing upward. Applied to pentatonic or diatonic groupings, broken groups create longer, more interesting melodic contours than straight scalar runs and are a step toward idiomatic jazz phrasing.

## Appearances

- **[[../videos/0paCMVrPm_8|Five Levels of Pentatonic Scale Practice]]** [▶ 20:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0paCMVrPm_8&t=1253s) — introduced as Level 3, after students can play the pentatonic in straight four-note groupings (Level 2). Described as the moment where "the pentatonic sound truly emerges." Each four-note group is given an arch shape, and the groups are linked into longer phrases before being combined with linear runs in Level 4.
