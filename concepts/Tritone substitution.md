# Tritone substitution

A tritone substitution (or tritone sub) is a harmonic device where a dominant 7 chord is played a half step above the target chord instead of the traditional V-I or dominant-tonic relationship. Essentially, any chord can be approached by a dominant 7 chord positioned one half step above it. The tritone sub creates tension that resolves into the target chord, adding harmonic color and sophistication. It is particularly effective when voiced as a dominant 7 sharp 11 (Lydian dominant), which maintains harmonic ambiguity while building anticipation. Tritone subs work in any musical context—5-1 cadences, II-V-I progressions, or approaching non-dominant chords—and are a cornerstone of modern jazz improvisation and comping.
