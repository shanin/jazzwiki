# Spread Voicing

A spread voicing employs a two-hand structure where the left hand voices the harmonic foundation (typically a block chord, cluster, or fourths voicing) and the right hand plays octaves with an interior note—typically a fourth or fifth above the lower octave note. This creates a wide, spacious sonority that fills the register between the left and right hands. Spread voicings are particularly valuable in ensemble arranging because they produce thick, full sounds that cover substantial harmonic ground without muddiness. The structural flexibility of spread voicings allows the left-hand voicing to remain stable while the right-hand spread moves, creating dynamic textural variety. Red Garland's distinctive approach maintains a perfect fifth as the interior note regardless of the harmonic context, a technique that prioritizes consistency and ease over strict diatonic conformity.
