# Phrygian dominant scale

The Phrygian dominant scale is the fifth mode of harmonic minor, combining a dominant seventh quality with a Phrygian character. For example, E Phrygian dominant is the fifth mode of A harmonic minor: it contains the pitches E, F, G sharp, A, B, C, D. When a dominant seventh chord approaches a minor chord (e.g., E7 to A minor seven), Phrygian dominant provides a darker, more minor-oriented color than unaltered Mixolydian. The flat nine and flat thirteen intervals of Phrygian dominant create weight and gravity that guide the ear toward the minor destination chord. While unaltered Mixolydian technically works over any dominant seventh, Phrygian dominant is the more common and sophisticated choice in modern jazz when the target chord is minor. This distinction between Mixolydian (dominant to major) and Phrygian dominant (dominant to minor) is one of the core improvisational decisions in jazz harmony.
