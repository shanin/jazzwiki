# Diatonic seventh chords

Diatonic seventh chords are seventh chords built by stacking every other note within a single key's scale. In C major, these seven chords are C major seven, D minor seven, E minor seven, F major seven, G dominant seven, A minor seven, and B minor seven flat five. Each diatonic seventh chord corresponds to one of the seven modes: Ionian, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Aeolian, and Locrian respectively. Understanding diatonic seventh chords is the foundation of jazz harmony because it frames the question "what scale do I play on this chord?" If a chord is built from a key's scale, then the improviser plays that corresponding mode. This approach eliminates guesswork and grounds improvisation in harmonic coherence. When reading a lead sheet, the first assumption is that all chords are diatonic until secondary dominants or modulations suggest otherwise. Building and internalizing diatonic seventh chords in multiple keys is one of the most efficient ways to unlock jazz improvisation.
