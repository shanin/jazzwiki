# Dominant 7 sharp 9

A dominant 7 sharp 9 chord combines the dominant 7 shell (major 3rd and minor 7th) with a raised 9th (sharp 9, spelled as D sharp or E flat), creating a distinctive harsh and bluesy sound. The sharp 9 alteration is one of the most recognizable altered dominant extensions in jazz, giving the chord an edgy, almost angular quality. This voicing is highly effective for comping in blues contexts and as a substitute for standard dominant 7 voicings when a more exotic or rebellious harmonic color is desired. When voiced using Root Shell Pretty, the sharp 9 replaces the natural 9, while the 5th provides harmonic grounding.
