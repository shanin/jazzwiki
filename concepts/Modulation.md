# Modulation

Modulation is a temporary or permanent shift from one key center to another within a musical piece. Unlike secondary dominants, which support the underlying diatonic framework, modulation establishes a new tonal center for a passage. In standards like How High the Moon, modulations occur when a sequence of chords suggests a different key: for example, G minor to C7 to F major indicates a shift to F major (where G minor is the diatonic ii chord). The recognizing modulations requires identifying chord sequences that belong to keys other than the home key and understanding where they resolve. Once a musician recognizes that a passage is in F major rather than G major, they can simplify their thinking by playing within the F major scale (a single color palette) rather than analyzing each chord individually. Modulation is common in jazz standards and great American songbook tunes, adding harmonic interest and surprise. Understanding modulation as a shift in key center—rather than as a collection of unrelated chords—unlocks interpretation and improvisation.
