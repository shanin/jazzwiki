# The Big Four

The Big Four is a foundational rhythmic pattern that emerged in New Orleans and became central to the birth of jazz. In a straight march rhythm, the beat is evenly distributed across all four beats. The Big Four, introduced by Buddy Bolden and other early New Orleans musicians, shifts the emphasis to the fourth beat, creating an accent where the drum and cymbal hit together. This shift from even pulse to syncopated accent gave early jazz its distinctive lilt and forward momentum, distinguishing it from march and ragtime traditions. The Big Four can also be understood as an "anticipation of one"—the fourth-beat accent throws the rhythm off the grid and creates anticipation for the returning downbeat. Understanding the Big Four is foundational to understanding how jazz rhythm works and why swing feel matters more than harmonic complexity.
