# Lydian dominant scale

The Lydian dominant scale is a major scale with a raised fourth degree (sharp 11) and dominant seventh intervals. Unlike modes of the major scale, the Lydian dominant comes from the melodic minor family, which gives it an ambiguous, unsettled quality. It does not imply a specific major key center, making it ideal for harmonic situations that require ambiguity or tension. In the context of tritone substitutions, the Lydian dominant scale is the preferred choice because it prevents the tritone sub from sounding like it is establishing a new key; instead, it creates a suspended, tension-filled sound that resolves clearly into the target chord. For example, D flat Lydian dominant approaching C major contains a G natural (tied to C) rather than G flat (the third of G flat major), maintaining harmonic coherence while adding sophistication.
