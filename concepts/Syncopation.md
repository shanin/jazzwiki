# Syncopation

Syncopation is the practice of placing rhythmic emphasis on weak beats or off-beat positions, disrupting the regular pulse of the measure. In jazz, syncopation is inseparable from swing and is rooted in African-American musical traditions. Classic syncopated patterns include the "big four"—an accent on the fourth beat of a march that creates momentum and anticipation. Syncopation can be understood technically (as specific beat placements or groupings), but it is internalized and felt rather than calculated. Musicians learn syncopation through listening and singing rhythmic patterns, not through analytical counting. The syncopated rhythm appears "off the grid" because it sits outside the regular pulse, but this apparent displacement is what creates the forward-moving, conversational quality of jazz.
