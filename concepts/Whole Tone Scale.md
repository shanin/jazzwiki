# Whole Tone Scale

The whole tone scale is a six-note scale built entirely of whole step intervals, creating a symmetrical sonority that is ambiguous in tonality and has a dreamy, floating quality. Because the scale has no leading tone and every tritone relationship is symmetrical, it avoids the sense of traditional harmonic resolution and tension-release that defines major and minor tonality. In jazz, the whole tone scale is often used over dominant seven chords or in contexts where an unsettled, spacious, or impressionistic sound is desired. Its ergonomical properties on the keyboard—particularly when white keys and black keys are separated—make it a popular choice for showy scalar passages that sound exotic yet remain comfortable to play at speed.
