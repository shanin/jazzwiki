# Broken ninth chords

A broken ninth chord is an arpegiated voicing of a ninth chord (root, third, fifth, seventh, ninth) where the notes are distributed across the range rather than stacked in close position. The term "broken" refers to the non-sequential nature of the voicing—the notes do not necessarily ascend from lowest to highest but are intentionally displaced across octaves to create a specific melodic shape and harmonic character. The third is often placed an octave higher, surrounding the chord tones with space rather than density. This voicing structure is fundamental to bebop language and serves as an efficient way to outline harmonic changes; it remains ubiquitous in post-bebop and modern jazz. Broken ninth patterns can be applied to chords of any quality—dominant sevenths, major sevenths, minor sevenths—and are valuable tools for both comping and melodic improvisation.
