# Modal interchange

Modal interchange (also called borrowed chords or mixture) is a harmonic technique where chords are borrowed from parallel keys, most commonly from the parallel minor. Instead of modulating to a new key, modal interchange allows a progression to include chords from outside the original key while maintaining a stable tonal center. In a major key, borrowing from the parallel minor creates darker, more sophisticated harmonic colors. For example, in C major, the vi chord (Am) can be replaced with the borrowed vi from C minor (Ab major), or the simple progression I-vi-IV-V can be transformed by taking the vi, IV, and V up a minor third into the parallel minor key (E flat major) while keeping the tonic (C) constant. This creates a progression like C major, C minor, A flat major 7, B flat 7—all while the tonal center remains C. Modal interchange is fundamental to modern jazz harmony and appears frequently in standards and contemporary compositions.
