# Flat 3 diminished

The flat 3 diminished (also known as the tonic diminished) is a chord substitution technique where a diminished chord is built a half step above the target chord. For a 6 chord in a 3-6-2-5 progression, a diminished chord approaches the 2 chord (e.g., an F# diminished approaching G minor 7). This substitution creates beautiful inner voice movement and is noted as one of the most enjoyable voicings to play on the piano. The flat 3 diminished is particularly underexplored and under-discussed in contemporary jazz practice, despite its long history in jazz harmony. It represents a sophisticated approach to harmonic color that relies on the diminished chord's ambiguity and voice-leading qualities. The voicing maintains the 1-3-5-7 structure through hinging, allowing for smooth and natural chord progressions.
