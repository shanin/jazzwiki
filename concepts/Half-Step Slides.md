# Half-Step Slides

A chromatic approach technique in which a melodic figure or voicing shape is displaced a half step below (or above) the target and then resolved back. Applied to pentatonic groups, this creates a momentary "out" sound that quickly resolves, giving phrases a hip, modern quality without departing far from the underlying harmony.

## Appearances

- **[[../videos/0paCMVrPm_8|Five Levels of Pentatonic Scale Practice]]** [▶ 37:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0paCMVrPm_8&t=2268s) — introduced as Level 5, the final and most rhythmically active variation. A four-note C minor pentatonic group is slid down a half step into B minor pentatonic territory, then returned to C minor. For pianists, the technique is also demonstrated as a voicing move using quartal shapes. Maness notes that half-step slides "work brilliantly with pentatonic scales" because the interval structure of the pentatonic tolerates the brief displacement naturally.
