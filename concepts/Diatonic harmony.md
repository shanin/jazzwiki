# Diatonic harmony

Diatonic harmony refers to harmonic relationships and chord progressions that stay within a single major or minor key, using only the pitches available in that key's scale. In the key of C major, diatonic harmony includes all chords built from the white keys: C major, D minor, E minor, F major, G major, A minor, and B diminished. Diatonic harmony creates a sense of coherence and consonance because all pitches share the same tonal center. When musicians step outside diatonic harmony—such as by introducing a tritone substitution—they create tension and instability that can be resolved back into the diatonic framework. Understanding the interplay between diatonic harmony and chromatic substitutions is fundamental to jazz harmony, where musicians use non-diatonic chords as temporary tools to add color and sophistication before returning to harmonic stability.
