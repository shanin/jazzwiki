# 5 Chords EVERYONE Plays (And You Should Too)

**Presenter:** Adam Maness | **Channel:** Open Studio
**Duration:** 19:19 | **YouTube:** [▶ Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hiQdy_q-F2U)

## Overview

Adam Maness introduces five essential chord voicings that every jazz pianist should know: major seven, dominant seven, minor seven, half diminished, and diminished seven. Each chord receives two rootless voicing variations designed for rhythm section playing. The lesson emphasizes that these "cookie cutter" voicings, while common and generic, sound beautiful and are used by the greatest pianists in jazz.

- [[Mulgrew Miller]] — cited as using these voicing techniques

## Concepts Introduced

- [[Cookie cutter chords]] — universal voicings that work in any harmonic context
- [[Rootless Voicings]] — chords without the root, designed for rhythm section comping
- [[Major seven chord]] — foundational voicing type with major third and major seventh
- [[Dominant seven chord]] — tension-creating voicing with major third and minor seventh
- [[Minor seven chord]] — stable voicing with minor third and minor seventh
- [[Half diminished chord]] — transitional voicing with minor third, diminished fifth, and minor seventh
- [[Diminished seven chord]] — symmetrical voicing with minor third, diminished fifth, and double flat seven
- [[Tritone substitution]] — harmonic substitute a half step above the target chord
- [[Altered dominant]] — dominant with raised or lowered extensions for chromatic color

## Practice Notes

All voicings shown are rootless, designed for two-handed comping with a rhythm section or bass player. Each chord type receives two voicing variations: a lower inversion and a higher inversion. The lesson works through multiple keys (C, F, B flat) to help lock these shapes into the fingers. Transposition across keys is essential to practical application.
