# 5 Levels of Pentatonics

**Presenter:** Adam Maness | **Channel:** Open Studio
**Duration:** 45:30 | **YouTube:** [▶ Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0paCMVrPm_8)

## Overview

A comprehensive 45-minute guided practice session on the C minor pentatonic scale structured into five systematically increasing levels of complexity. Each level adds a new rhythmic or intervallic concept while remaining rooted in the same foundational material. The instructor emphasizes practicing with intentionality and connection to music rather than as mechanical exercise, treating all practice as serious performance.

## Concepts Introduced

- [[Pentatonic Scales]] — the C minor pentatonic with five pitches (root, minor third, fourth, fifth, flat seventh), applied as foundational material
- [[Linear Scale Practice]] — playing the pentatonic scale up and down in continuous form
- [[Simple Grouping]] — breaking the pentatonic scale into four-note groups as a foundation for higher-level work
- [[Broken Groups]] — rearranging note groupings within the pentatonic to create varied patterns
- [[Combining Elements]] — weaving together linear scales, simple groups, and broken groups into longer phrases
- [[Half-Step Slides]] — approaching pentatonic notes from a half-step above, a technique especially effective with pentatonics
- [[Intentional Practice]] — treating all practice with the seriousness and musicality of a live performance
- [[Imagery and Listening in Practice]] — imagining a favorite musician to inform tone and phrasing before executing
- [[Systematic Complexity]] — progressing through structured levels that each build on and reuse previous work

## Practice Notes

**Tempo:** 88 BPM throughout

**Material:** All exercises use the C minor pentatonic scale

**Level 1:** Emphasize evenness of tone and duration across each note; no accents

**Level 2 and beyond:** Apply musicality and intention; imagine a favorite musician playing the material

**Duration:** 45 minutes total, with a mindfulness break around the 30-minute mark

**Mindfulness break:** Includes stretching, deep breathing, and reflection on how material might apply to tunes in the student's repertoire
