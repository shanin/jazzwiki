# Voicing A Standard

**Presenter:** Adam Maness | **Channel:** Open Studio
**Duration:** 21:07 | **YouTube:** [▶ Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ktm1Q0odH8)

## Overview

This comprehensive lesson explores three distinct chord voicing structures for jazz pianists: rooted voicings for solo piano, rootless left-hand voicings for accompanying rhythm sections, and magic voicings for band comping. Using Tad Dameron's "Lady Bird" as the primary example, the instructor demonstrates how pianists adapt their voicing approach based on their role in an ensemble.

## Tunes & Standards Referenced

- *[[Lady Bird]]* (Tad Dameron) — comprehensive voicing study applied throughout all three voicing systems
- *[[So What]]* — referenced as a voicing model for certain chord structures

## Musicians & Influences Referenced

- [[Peter Martin]] — source of an unconventional voicing used in the magic voicing section
- [[Red Garland]] — rhythm style referenced for comping applications

## Concepts Introduced

- [[Chord Voicings]] — the fundamental technique of arranging chord tones in different registers and patterns
- [[Root Shell Pretty]] — a systematic approach to building rooted voicings from the bottom up using root, shell (third/seventh), and a melodic extension
- [[Three-Zone Concept]] — separating piano texture into root zone (left hand), chord zone (middle fingers), and melody zone (right hand)
- [[Rootless Voicings]] — chord structures that omit the root to create a complementary accompaniment texture when a bassist is present
- [[Magic Voicings]] — sophisticated rootless comping voicings built on pentatonic scales with upper-structure extensions

## Practice Notes

All three voicing systems are demonstrated over "Lady Bird" with specific chord-by-chord application. The instructor emphasizes:
- Keeping rooted voicings tight to avoid overly wide intervals
- Understanding how guide tones (third and seventh) create smooth voice leading between chord changes
- Building major voicings in perfect fourths from either the third or seventh of the chord
- Using pentatonic scales as the foundation for magic voicing construction
- Applying the Red Garland rhythm pattern (anticipating changes on beat 4) for effective comping
