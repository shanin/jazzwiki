# Voicing A Standard

**Presenter:** Adam Maness | **Channel:** Open Studio
**Duration:** 21:07 | **YouTube:** [▶ Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ktm1Q0odH8)

## Overview
This lesson explores three essential voicing structures used to accompany jazz standards: rooted voicings for solo piano performance, rootless left-hand voicings for playing within a rhythm section, and rootless two-handed "magic" voicings for comping with a full band. All three systems are demonstrated over Tad Dameron's "Lady Bird," providing a comprehensive look at how pianists adapt their voicing approach based on their role in the ensemble.

## Tunes & Standards Referenced
- *Lady Bird* (Tad Dameron) — used throughout as the vehicle for exploring all three voicing systems

## Musicians & Influences Referenced
- Peter Martin — a voicing concept is borrowed from his approach
- Red Garland — a rhythmic comping style is referenced for the magic voicing section

## Concepts Introduced
- [[Rooted Voicing]] — a voicing system that includes the root of the chord, used when playing solo piano
- [[Root Shell Pretty]] — a structural framework for building rooted voicings from the bottom up: root, then the third and/or seventh (the shell), then extension notes for color
- [[Rootless Left-Hand Voicing]] — a voicing system built from guide tones and extensions without the root, used when accompanying a rhythm section
- [[Magic Voicing]] — a rootless, two-handed comping voicing system designed to sit well in ensemble settings with dense harmonic color and minimal note repetition
- [[Three-Zone Concept]] — organizing the keyboard into three registers: root in the left hand, melody in the upper right hand, and harmony in the middle zone
- [[Guide Tones]] — the shell notes (third and seventh) of a chord, used as anchors in rootless voicings
- [[Perfect Fourth Voicing]] — stacking notes in perfect fourths, particularly useful for building major chord voicings
- [[Pentatonic Scale Voicing]] — building magic voicings by spacing notes from a single pentatonic scale

## Practice Notes
- Work through the three voicing systems sequentially over "Lady Bird"
- For rooted voicings: keep hand span tight (no tenths unless you have larger hands); play the melody with fourth and fifth fingers while keeping the root in the left hand
- For rootless left-hand voicings: observe how guide tones move minimally between consecutive chords; notice when only a single note changes
- For magic voicings: learn to recognize patterns within pentatonic scales; build muscle memory by transposing through different keys
- Practice the Red Garland comping rhythm for a locked-in feel with the magic voicings
- Spend an entire practice session focusing on one voicing system across multiple tunes in your repertoire
