# Why Should I Bother Learning Scales?

**Presenter:** Adam Maness | **Channel:** Open Studio
**Duration:** 20:32 | **YouTube:** [▶ Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qbxvt1dXdYY)

## Overview

Adam Maness demonstrates why learning scales—specifically the modes of the major scale and diatonic seventh chords—is foundational to jazz improvisation. Rather than choosing individual notes, improvisers choose scales based on harmonic context. This lesson covers three core principles: diatonic seventh chords, secondary dominance, and the distinction between Mixolydian and Phrygian dominant sounds, all of which unlock the ability to answer the fundamental question: "What scale do I play on this chord?"

## Tunes & Standards Referenced

- *[[Time After Time]]* — analyzed to show how diatonic seventh chords and secondary dominants frame chord changes
- *[[How High the Moon]]* — examined for its use of modulation and secondary dominants within a single key context

## Musicians & Influences Referenced

- [[Adam Maness]] — presenter and instructor throughout

## Concepts Introduced

- [[Ionian]], [[Dorian]], [[Phrygian]], [[Aeolian]], [[Locrian]], [[Lydian]], [[Mixolydian]] — the seven modes of the major scale
- [[Diatonic seventh chords]] — seventh chords built from a single key's scale
- [[Secondary dominance]] — dominant seventh chords that resolve to non-tonic chords within a key
- [[Phrygian dominant scale]] — the fifth mode of harmonic minor, used for dominant-to-minor color
- [[Modulation]] — temporary key centers within a tune's framework

## Practice Notes

Build diatonic seventh chords in your key (e.g., C major: C△7, Dm7, Em7, F△7, G7, Am7, Bm7♭5). Play them as simple voicings to hear the consonance of staying in one key. Then add secondary dominants: A7→Dm7, B7→Em7, C7→F△7, D7→G7, E7→Am7, F#7→Bm7♭5. When approaching a major chord, use Mixolydian (unaltered dominant). When approaching a minor chord, use Phrygian dominant (♭9, ♭13 color). Recognize modulations in standards by identifying chord sequences that suggest a new key center (e.g., Gm7-C7-F in a tune centered on C major suggests a temporary shift to F).
