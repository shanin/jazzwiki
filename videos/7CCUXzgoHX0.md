# 4 Flashy Runs (That Are Easier Than You Think)

**Presenter:** Adam Maness | **Channel:** Open Studio
**Duration:** 13:58 | **YouTube:** [▶ Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7CCUXzgoHX0)

## Overview

Adam Maness introduces four pianistic runs that sound flashy and difficult but are actually ergonomical and easy to play fast. These runs, made famous by pianists like Art Tatum, are presented in E flat major and designed to fit naturally under the hands, with practical fingering patterns and practice methods for developing speed and control.

## Tunes & Standards Referenced

None explicitly named.

## Musicians & Influences Referenced

- [[Art Tatum]] — legendary pianist famous for playing these types of flashy runs
- [[Oscar Peterson]] — another renowned pianist who employs similar running passages

## Concepts Introduced

- [[Flashy Runs]] — showy scalar passages that sound technically impressive but use ergonomical hand positions
- [[Blocking Practice]] — a practice technique where all notes of one hand are played simultaneously before breaking them up into the intended rhythm
- [[Dominant 7 chord]] — the five chord used as setup for runs #1 and #3
- [[Whole Tone Scale]] — the basis for run #3, creating a dreamy, spacious sound

## Practice Notes

All four runs are presented in E flat major, a key where they lay particularly well. Adam emphasizes:
- Ergonomical fingering patterns that prioritize hand comfort over traditional rules
- The repeatable nature of each run shape, allowing "grip it and rip it" speed once the basic shape is internalized
- Metronome practice starting slowly, with gradual tempo increases
- Using blocking practice (playing hand together, then broken up) to develop hand position before attempting full speed
- The role of the sustain pedal in creating a clean effect
- Transposing runs to other keys to find which work well and which don't
