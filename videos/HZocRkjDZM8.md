# Why Should I Care About Tritone Subs?

**Presenter:** Adam Maness | **Channel:** Open Studio
**Duration:** 27:51 | **YouTube:** [▶ Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HZocRkjDZM8)

## Overview

Tritone substitutions are a powerful harmonic device that allows jazz musicians to add tension and sophistication to any chord progression. This lesson explores what tritone subs are, how they function as half-step upper approaches, which scales and voicings work best with them, and practical methods for incorporating them into improvisation. The focus is on the Lydian dominant sound and hands-on practice over a 2-5-1 progression.

## Tunes & Standards Referenced

- *All the Things You Are* — example of tritone substitution as hinted rather than complete substitution

## Musicians & Influences Referenced

- [[Peter Martin]] — jazz pianist known for sophisticated harmonic approaches
- [[Adam Maness]] — instructor and presenter

## Concepts Introduced

- [[Tritone substitution]] — dominant 7 chord a half step above the target chord
- [[Lydian dominant scale]] — major scale with raised 4th and dominant 7 intervals
- [[Dominant 7 sharp 11]] — the primary voicing for tritone subs
- [[2-5-1 progression]] — fundamental jazz progression used for practice
- [[Diatonic harmony]] — harmonic relationships within a key
- [[Tension and release]] — how tritone subs create and resolve harmonic tension

## Practice Notes

- Start slow with quarter-note pulse
- Work with white keys (C major scale) on all chords except the tritone sub
- Use Lydian dominant sound to avoid establishing a new key center
- Apply tritone subs sparingly; they are seasoning, not the main dish
- Tritone subs can be applied to any chord, not just the V chord
- Bass player does not need to play the tritone sub for it to work effectively
- Approach any chord with a dominant 7 sharp 11 from a half step above

### [07:26] Scales & Chords for Tritone Subs — Lydian Dominant — [▶](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HZocRkjDZM8&t=446s)

[07:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HZocRkjDZM8&t=446s) The primary voicing for tritone subs is a [[Dominant 7 sharp 11]] chord, played over the [[Lydian dominant scale]]. This scale works best with tritone subs because it is not a mode of the major scale; it belongs to the melodic minor scale. This gives tritone subs an ambiguous quality that prevents them from sounding like they are establishing a new key center.

[08:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HZocRkjDZM8&t=507s) When playing a tritone sub like Db7 sharp 11 approaching C major, the Lydian dominant scale (Db, Eb, F, G natural, Ab, Bb, Cb) does not contain G flat, the third of the target chord's relative key. Instead, the G natural in the scale ties the tritone sub back to the target key of C major, maintaining cohesion. This is why Lydian dominant is the preferred sound for tritone subs: it creates harmonic ambiguity while still resolving clearly.
