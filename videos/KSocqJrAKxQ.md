# Bill Evans Chords | Guided Practice Session™ with Adam Maness

**Presenter:** Adam Maness | **Channel:** Open Studio
**Duration:** 31:02 | **YouTube:** [▶ Watch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KSocqJrAKxQ)

## Overview

Adam Maness explores the sophisticated chord voicings and harmonic movement of Bill Evans through a simple three-bar cadence and turnaround. The lesson progresses through four keys, combining functional harmony with rich, densely voiced chords inspired by Jack Riley's analysis of Bill Evans' work. Pianists and guitarists learn to apply these voicings in ballad and medium tempos.

## Tunes & Standards Referenced

- *[[I Fall in Love Too Easily]]* — inspired arrangements with sophisticated cadences
- *[[Days of Wine and Roses]]* — context for turnaround use
- *[[Have You Met Miss Jones]]* — context for turnaround use
- *[[My Foolish Heart]]* — ending arrangement example

## Musicians & Influences Referenced

- [[Bill Evans]] — the harmonic framework and voicing style being taught
- [[Jack Riley]] — theorist whose analysis of Bill Evans' harmony inspired these voicings

## Concepts Introduced

- [[2-5-1 progression]] — the foundational chord progression underlying the cadence
- [[Tritone substitution]] — diminished harmony concepts
- [[Voice Leading]] — chromatic movement in the bass and upper voices
- [[Chord Voicings]] — the dense, four-way close voicings central to Bill Evans' style
- [[Dominant 7 sharp 11]] — the "money voicing" used throughout
- [[Sugar Chords]] — the special D flat seven, sharp 11 voicing
- [[Tension and release]] — how sus chords resolve to create richness

## Practice Notes

The lesson uses ballad tempo throughout, with optional rolling of chords for pianists with smaller hand spans. The four keys practiced are F, G, A flat, and B flat. Adam emphasizes transposition as a learning tool and explores multiple voicing interpretations of each chord quality, particularly the sus-to-dominant resolution.
